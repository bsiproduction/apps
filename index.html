ShopCall · Web app · shop @30 · Android 1.4

The shop, off paper.

ShopCall is how the crew runs the equipment shop from a phone or a desk. Gear orders for each event, picking the gear off the shelf, checking it back in, loans, stock counts and the shop notice board, all built on the shop's own Google Sheets.

  1. Today

    Opens on what needs you.

    Each role gets its own Today: what to act on, what's coming, and whether anything is broken. When the list is empty it says so. When it couldn't check, it says that instead of pretending all is clear.

  2. Orders

    Start an order from the calendar.

    An event on the calendar with “No order yet” gets a Start its order button. ShopCall copies the order template and registers the event as a draft.

  3. Order Builder

    Type it the way you'd say it.

    One search box: 6 sm58 means six SM58 microphones. It shows how many are actually free on those dates, with other events and loans already subtracted. Bundles, a “usually goes along” list, and fixes for shortages (rent one in, or swap in a stand-in). Then print the case labels and Release the order to the shop floor.

  4. Shop Board

    The bulletin board, the shopping list, done today.

    Post to the board from any phone. Shopping-list items move from open to ordered to received. Inspections and cleaning show up under Done today.

Picking the gear, line by line

The phone screen walks the order item by item as a technician pulls gear off the shelf. Ring means not yet, dot means pulled, truck means it's on the truck. If the order changes part way through, the line says so and asks for a Got it.

Check-in when the gear comes back

Count each line back in, report damage, add a note. Anything short or missing can go straight to the shopping list.

Three roles

Technicians on the shop floor, the office with Today, Gigs, Board and Tasks, and the desk with orders, approvals, errors and settings.

Notes and feedback

Anyone can leave a Note. It stays on each person's Today until they tap Seen. Send feedback goes to a shared inbox at the desk.

6 am digest

A morning email with the same checks as the app's needs-you list, plus a report after each event and Google Chat alerts.

Truck plan on the wall

Loader's truck plan shows up in ShopCall to scale, nose to doors, and on a big screen in the shop that refreshes itself.

On every phone

One crew link, bsiproduction.github.io. A BSI tile on the iPhone home screen, an Android app with no address bar, and a built-in QR scanner that opens an item or a case from the tag stuck to it.

Each page wears its stone

Today is sandstone, Gigs are lapis, Inventory is jade, Approvals are amethyst. The Mineral header palette tells you where you are before you read a word.

An event, from calendar to truck

What actually happens in the app, in order.

  1. No order yetThe event lands on the calendar. Today offers Start its order.
  2. Build itSearch, bundles, fix the shortages, one event per case.
  3. ReleasePrint case labels. The technician's Today now shows Start pull.
  4. PickRing to dot, line by line, as the gear comes off the shelf. Loader plans the truck alongside.

StandBy · Mac · 0.2.15 · Companion module 1.0.4

One Mac runs the show. Every GO on the Space bar.

StandBy is for the person calling the show: the one who says “go” for every lighting change, video roll and walk-on. One Mac holds the plan. Every other laptop, tablet and phone on the Wi-Fi follows along in a browser, with nothing to install. A second Mac runs the teleprompter.

  1. Caller

    StandBy, GO.

    The cards across the top show what's next, what's happening now with its time left, and whether the show is running early or late. Space is GO, ⌘Z backs one up, ⌘B is the big clock. Below it, the whole plan by department: video, audio, lighting.

  2. Display

    A backstage screen that says it plainly.

    /display is for a big screen in the wings or the green room: Show on time, what's happening now with its clock, and what's next with everything that goes with it. Each screen can be arranged by name from any laptop.

  3. Timer

    A countdown the speaker can see.

    /timer is a big countdown for the person on stage. /script scrolls the speaker's script in step with the show.

  4. Prompter

    Paste the script. Mark the cues.

    Paste the speaker's script from a Google Doc or Word. A ⟦12⟧ mark shows where cue 12 falls in the script, so the caller sees “N lines to go”. Mirror mode for teleprompter glass, any screen shape, and a speaker timer you can drag to size.

  5. Talent screen

    What the speaker sees.

    Big, clean, and locked down in Show mode so nothing can pop up in front of them. Practice mode rehearses privately while the crew screens stop following. Pace replays the rehearsal's reading speed.

Follow on any phone

Scan the QR code on the caller's screen, pick your department, and see what's next for you, what's happening now and what's coming up. Editors get /edit with a PIN and can fix the plan but never fire a GO.

From the Google Sheet

Write the run of show in the BSI template sheet. Check it, Send to Host, and Pull Show Results afterwards to see how the timing went. A plain CSV file works with no internet.

Messages and banners

M sends a silent text to every crew screen, and they can answer. Green banners close themselves after 8 seconds. Red ones wait for an OK.

Rehearsal mode

Before the first GO, times are recorded without counting as the real show. Rehearsal is violet, next is amber, now is green, late is red.

Stream Deck

A Stream Deck is a pad of physical buttons. StandBy's Companion module gives it a 15-key layout coloured like the caller screen. End Show needs a 2-second hold. A button can fire a GO but never change the plan.

Finds the caller on its own

The Prompter lists “StandBy on this network” by itself, no addresses to type. A caller it can see but can't reach shows greyed out with what to check.

Never updates during a show

Check for Updates… pulls small signed packages from BSI's own feed. It waits until the show is over, and Go Back to Previous Version… undoes it.

  1. Send to HostIn the Sheet's StandBy menu: check the plan, then Send to Host.

PatchMap · Mac · 1.15.0

Draw the rig once.

PatchMap is where a technician draws the equipment plan for an event: every console, camera, speaker and the cables between them. That one drawing then becomes the cable list, the cable labels, the pack list and the gear order.

  1. Schematic ⌘1

    Drop gear. Click port to port.

    Pull equipment from the library into racks, tables and zones (front of house, stage, monitor world), or onto a venue map. Click a socket on one device, then a socket on another, to run a cable. Zoomed out, each device shows its name and how many sockets are in use.

  2. Inspector

    Every socket, every label.

    Zoom in and the sockets come back. Plugging the wrong connector type warns you, with Patch anyway for adapters. Cable ID, label and length live in the Inspector.

  3. Racks

    Open a rack, see what's inside.

    A rack is a case full of equipment. A dashed ring on a rack's socket means the cable is connected inside. Built racks save to the library and come back whole next time.

  4. Patch sheet ⌘2

    One line per cable.

    From, to, connector type and label, with a tick box for connecting it on site. Assign cable IDs…, Print labels, Copy as text, or Print / PDF the whole pack.

  5. Packlist ⌘3

    Everything to pack, with Out and Back.

    Grouped by rack and zone. Live stock counts come from ShopCall, and a red “N short” marker shows what the shop can't cover before you send the order.

Loader · Mac · 0.4.0

Plan the truck before anyone lifts a case.

Loader packs an event's road cases into a truck in 3D, using BSI's own case library and the rules that come with each case. Then it prints the labels, the checklist and the unloading order.

  1. The load

    A 26' box, packed nose to doors.

    Drag a case in, or press + to put it in the next open spot. R turns it, T tips it, N pushes it to the nose. The list on the right is the loading order the crew reads from.

  2. Colour by what's inside

    See the speakers from across the shop.

    Every physical case is linked to what's packed inside it. Switch on Colour by What's Inside and the truck lights up by group: speakers, amplifier racks, consoles, cable.

  3. From above

    Four views, one plan.

    3D, Above, Side and Doors. The PDF prints all four, with a loading step by step and an Unloading at the venue page: last in, first out, grouped by where each case goes in the venue.

  4. Open Shop Order

    The gear order becomes the To pack list.

    File › Open Shop Order… reads the event's order from ShopCall. Pack cases one at a time with Enter, or Pack the rest and flip through the best three plans.

  5. Checklist

    In and Out, on paper or on a phone.

    The checklist lists every case with an In box and an Out box. Send Pack Numbers writes each case's truck, position and loading step back to the order, so ShopCall can draw the same truck.

Road case types, 77 pieces

The 200, 300 and 350 series cases, rack cases and speaker carts, straight from a walk around the shop, with size, weight and how many BSI owns.

Rules the shop already has

This side up, nothing on top, fragile, on wheels, wheel cups, stays on the floor. A 300-series case tips onto its end, never its side.

Blocks the bad stack

No overlaps, nothing through a wall, nothing on a no-stack case. Wheeled cases only sit in matching wheel cups. Warns on overhang and heavy over light.

Which truck do I need?

Sprinter van, 16', 24' and 26' box trucks, 48' and 53' trailers, with the E-track rails that straps lock into. Choose a truck trial-packs every one and picks the smallest that holds everything.

Import from a walkthrough

Paste the transcript: “Amp rack A, 30 by 24 by 40, 220 pounds, this side up.” Loader reads it into the case library, or takes a CSV.

Load bars and straps

Suggest Load Bars and Straps places them on the rails so nothing shifts on the road. B and S add them by hand.

Pack labels

Sheet labels striped in the event's tape colour, with a QR code that opens the case in ShopCall.

The plan PDF, the checklist and the labels land in the order's Drive folder.

TrussTape · Mac · 0.1.1 Beta

Stick the plot on the truss.

Stage lights hang from truss, the metal beams above a stage. TrussTape takes the lighting designer's drawing and prints a full-size paper tape on an ordinary receipt printer. Stick it on the truss and every light's label sits exactly where that light hangs.

  1. Positions

    Every truss and boom, with its lights.

    Run the export script in Vectorworks (the drawing program) and TrussTape opens the plan. Pick Truss 1, tick the label lines you want, and the pages preview as they'll print, so no label is cut in half at a join.

  2. The tape

    Unit, channel, address, colour, purpose.

    Each label prints on a ruler in feet and inches or metres, so the crew can measure as they go. Zero at the centre of the truss or the start of the tape. Flip it to read from the other end. Cut at the ✂ lines and butt the pages together.

AS HUNGSample ballroom plan, 24 lights, 3 trusses

From Vectorworks, without touching the drawing

The export script writes every light and every hanging position to a small file and opens TrussTape. It changes nothing in the drawing. Or drop in the spreadsheet from Export Instrument Data.

1:1

Full size, or a pocket strip

1:2 to 1:12 for a reference you can carry, from the same plan.

Any roll

80 mm receipt, 58 mm, Dymo 2¼" continuous, or custom. Page length and paper loss are adjustable.

Feed calibration

Printers don't feed paper perfectly. Print a 500 mm strip, measure it, type the number, and every tape after that is stretched to match. A percent off is inches over a 40' truss.

Print or PDF

Print this position, Print all, Save PDF, Save all as PDF. Any printer your Mac can see, with a custom roll size.

First build

0.1.1 is the first test build. It prints to PDF today and is waiting on its first run on a thermal printer and its first run of the script inside Vectorworks. Feedback from that test shapes 0.2.

Plot to truss

The whole thing, from the drawing to the hang.

  1. ExportTools › Scripts › Run Script… › TrussTape Export.py.
  2. Pick the positionTruss 1, and the label lines you want on it.
  3. 80 mm at 1:1Print this position.
  4. Cut and buttTear at the ✂ lines and join the pages end to end.

Under all of them

One kit. Four Mac apps. The same hands.

StandBy, PatchMap, Loader and TrussTape share an updater, a feedback window, a manual and a design system, so learning one means you already know the others.

Signed self-updates

Check for Updates… pulls a small package from BSI's public feed. Each release is signed with a key that exists only on the release Mac. An app refuses anything whose signature, size or hash doesn't match. Go Back to Previous Version… is one click.

A manual in every app

Help › Manual, ⇧⌘/, with search, What's New and guided tours on the ? key.

Send Feedback…

One shared collector. It lands in the desk's inbox in ShopCall.

One look

Montserrat for words, IBM Plex Mono for numbers, light and dark, and one accent per app.

Apple Silicon

macOS 12 or later. Installed once from a Drive zip, then it updates itself.
