1 · PatchMap · Plan

Draw the rig once.

The rig is every piece of gear an event needs: consoles, cameras, speakers, screens, and the cables that connect them. In PatchMap, a technician draws it once, and that drawing becomes the cable list, the labels, the packing list and the gear order.

The drawing

Drag in the gear. Click to connect it.

Pick from a library of the gear BSI owns and drop it in place. Click one connection point, then another, and the cable is drawn.

  • Every device comes with its real connections
  • It warns you when two plugs won't fit together
The cable list

Print the cable list and the labels.

One line per cable: where it starts, where it ends and what it carries. On site, the crew ticks each one off as it's plugged in.

The order

Send the packing list straight to the shop.

Everything the event needs, grouped by where it goes. One click sends it to ShopCall to become the gear order.

Draw once, print everything

The drawing, cable list, labels and packing list all come from the same plan.

Catches mistakes early

Unlabeled cables and gear with nothing plugged in get flagged before load-in.

Labels in one click

Every cable gets a printed label that matches the list, so nothing gets plugged in wrong.

2 · ShopCall · Order the gear · web, iPhone, Android

Run the shop off paper.

Everything your event uses lives on BSI's shelves until show day. ShopCall is how the crew orders it, pulls it, loads it and checks it back in, with everyone looking at the same live list.

Order Builder

Build the gear list in minutes.

Type what you need the way you'd say it: to add six microphones, type 6 sm58 and press Enter. Every line shows how many are actually free on your dates, so a shortage shows up before anyone packs a truck.

Pull & check-in

Pull it line by line. Check it back in the same way.

“Pulling” is taking the gear off the shelves for your event. Technicians work through the order on their phones and mark each item as it's pulled, then loaded. When it comes back, they check it in the same way and log anything damaged or missing.

Today & the wall

Everyone knows what needs them.

Technicians, the office and the desk each open ShopCall to a home screen of what needs them today. A big screen in the shop shows every event going out and coming back, readable from across the room.

One live list

The desk, the office and the shop floor see the same order the moment it changes.

Built for phones

An app on iPhone and Android, with a scanner for the QR tag on every case.

Right view for each role

Technicians, the office and the desk each see what they need and nothing they don't.

3 · Loader · Pack the truck

Pack the truck before anyone lifts a case.

Everything for a show travels in road cases: sturdy boxes on wheels, some heavy, some fragile, some that can't be stacked. Loader plans exactly where each one goes in the truck, in 3D, before the crew starts loading.

3D plan

See the whole load, front to back.

Loader knows the size, weight and rules of every case BSI owns. It blocks unsafe stacks, finds the smallest truck that fits, and builds the loading order as you go.

What's inside

Spot the speakers from across the room.

Every case knows what's packed in it. Color the truck by contents and each group stands out: speakers, amplifiers, mixing consoles, cable.

Load-out

Hand the crew a checklist, not a guess.

Every case in loading order, with a box to tick going in and coming out. The same plan shows up on the crew's phones, so unloading at the venue goes in the right order too.

Smallest truck that fits

It tries every truck and picks the smallest one that holds the whole event.

Safe stacks only

Heavy on light, fragile under weight, cases that must stay upright: all blocked.

Labels and checklist

Every case gets a color-coded label for your event, plus a checklist for the crew.

4 · TrussTape · Hang the lights NEW

Stick the lighting plan on the truss.

Stage lights hang from truss, the metal beams above a stage. TrussTape turns the lighting designer's plan into a full-size paper tape. Stick it on the truss, and every light's label sits exactly where that light hangs.

The tape

Hang every light in the right spot, first time.

Each label says which light goes there and what it's for, printed along a ruler so the crew can measure as they go. It prints on an ordinary receipt printer.

From the plan

Straight from the designer's drawing.

Lighting plans are drawn in Vectorworks, a design program for stage and venue plans. One click exports the plan to TrussTape, which lists every truss and lets you choose what each label shows.

Measured to the inch

Labels land exactly where each light hangs, on a ruler the crew can read.

Any receipt printer

No special hardware. Print a whole truss in a few minutes.

Straight from the plan

Nothing is retyped, so what's on the tape matches the designer's drawing.

5 · StandBy · Call the show

Call the show. Every GO on the Space bar.

A live show runs on cues: the moment the lights change, a video rolls, a speaker walks on. One person, the show caller, says GO for each one. In StandBy the caller presses the Space bar for every GO, and everyone backstage sees where the show is, what's next and whether it's on time.

The caller

StandBy, GO.

The next cue waits in amber, on “standby”. Press Space and it goes live in green, with its clock running and the next cue lined up behind it. You always know if the show is running early or late.

Every screen

Change it once. Every screen updates.

Speakers run long. Doors open late. Change a cue and every crew member's phone, every backstage screen and the speaker's countdown update instantly. Crew just scan a QR code, nothing to install.

Teleprompter

The speaker's script and the cues, in step.

The script scrolls on a teleprompter in front of the speaker. The caller sees exactly how many lines are left before the next cue, so the lights change on the right word.

Every screen in sync

One computer runs the show. Everyone else just opens a link on the same Wi-Fi.

Always on time

Every cue has a clock, so you know if the show is early or late before anyone asks.

Safe on show day

Crew can follow and edit, but only the caller can move the show forward.

Under all five

Built to be trusted on show day.

Never changes mid-show

The apps only update between shows, and any update can be undone in one click.

Works on what you're holding

Laptops, tablets and phones all see the same plan. Most people just open a link.

Questions

Can I use these apps for my event?

Yes, by having BSI Production run it. The apps come with BSI's crew and aren't sold or licensed separately.

What kinds of events are they built for?

Corporate events, galas, conferences, campus events, concerts and installs. Anything with sound, lighting, video and a running order.

Does my team need to install anything?

No. Your team can follow the show from any phone or laptop by scanning a QR code or opening a link.

What happens when the plan changes at the last minute?

Change it once. Every phone and screen looking at that plan updates instantly, from the shop to backstage.

Does the show depend on the internet?

No. StandBy runs the show over the venue's local Wi-Fi. The shop tools use the internet, but they're done before doors open.

Who built them?

BSI Production's own crew, for the way BSI actually works. They replace spreadsheets, printouts and a lot of phone calls.
